Scientific Themes

Probing Quantum Matter & Chemistry Near Absolute Zero

Our research combines atomic physics techniques—Doppler laser cooling and radiofrequency ion trapping—with physical chemistry methodologies such as Velocity Map Imaging (VMI) and Time-of-Flight mass spectrometry.

Theme 01

Laser Cooling & Multi-Species Coulomb Crystals

We trap atomic calcium ions (40Ca+) in a linear Paul trap driven by radiofrequency fields. Driving the strong 4s 2S1/2 → 4p 2P1/2 transition at 397 nm alongside repumping on the 3d 2D3/2 → 4p 2P1/2 transition at 866 nm reduces ion kinetic temperatures below 10 mK, causing the ions to condense into ordered 3D Coulomb crystals imaged via EMCCD fluorescence microscopy.

Theme 02

Sympathetic Cooling & Cold Molecular Ion Spectroscopy

Because most molecular ions lack closed optical cycling transitions, direct laser cooling is prohibitive. By co-trapping molecular ions with laser-cooled Ca+, long-range Coulomb collisions sympathetically cool the molecular translational motion into the millikelvin regime, suppressing Doppler broadening and enabling high-precision vibrational overtone and photodissociation action spectroscopy.

Theme 03

Velocity Map Imaging (VMI) of Trapped Ions

Coupling a linear Paul trap to a Velocity Map Imaging spectrometer allows us to extract parent and daughter ions directly from the trap center onto a 2D position-sensitive detector. Electrostatic immersion lenses map ions with identical initial velocity vectors to the same point on the phosphor screen regardless of their spatial origin inside the trap.

Theme 04

State-Selected Cold Ion–Molecule Reactions

By introducing pulsed or continuous beams of rotationally cold or polar-selected neutral molecules into our trapped Coulomb crystals, we monitor chemical reactions in real time with single-ion sensitivity. We determine absolute rate coefficients, kinetic isotope effects, and product branching ratios relevant to cold interstellar clouds.

Methodology Deep Dive

Theoretical & Experimental Principles

Click on each module below to expand the physical principles governing ion confinement, sympathetic cooling, and velocity map imaging in our laboratory.

Earnshaw's theorem prohibits 3D trapping of charged particles using static electrostatic fields alone. Our segmented linear Paul trap applies a time-varying radiofrequency potential V0 cos(Ωt) to radial electrodes and static DC voltages Uend to endcap segments. The equations of motion map onto the canonical Mathieu differential equation parameterized by dimensionless stability parameters au and qu. Within the lowest stability region (q < 0.908), ions experience a harmonic radial effective pseudopotential whose depth scales inversely with ion mass m.

Because the radial pseudopotential depth is inversely proportional to mass, lighter sympathetically cooled molecular ions (mmol < mCa+) localize along the trap central axis, displacing fluorescing 40Ca+ ions into a hollow cylindrical shell. Conversely, heavier molecular ions (mmol > mCa+) experience weaker radial confinement and form an outer non-fluorescing ring around the bright Ca+ core. Comparing experimental EMCCD images against Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations yields non-destructive stoichiometry and temperature diagnostics.

By pulsing the trap rods or endcap electrodes in synchronization with an electrostatic immersion lens stack (repeller, extractor, and ground electrodes), the entire trapped ion ensemble is accelerated down a field-free Time-of-Flight tube. Arrival times provide mass-to-charge (m/z) identification via PMT traces, while fast high-voltage gating on the Chevron Microchannel Plate (MCP) detector isolates a single mass species for 2D spatial imaging. Inverse Abel transformation reconstructs the complete 3D velocity sphere of photodissociation or reaction products.
